# PRD Quality Review — grpc_network_gateway — declarative control plane

## Overall verdict

This is a strong PRD, and unusually so in the two dimensions that normally rot first: every FR carries a testable consequence with real bounds, and the omissions are stated with reasons rather than inferred — including one place (§7 NetworkPolicy) where the PRD publishes disconfirming evidence against its own delivered work. I spot-checked all eleven `[DELIVERED]` FRs against the repo and every claim holds, down to the `ON CONFLICT (local_ip)` upsert, the delete ordering, the `""`/`liveness` health-entry split, and the digest-pinned distroless base; brownfield accuracy is not a risk here.

What is at risk is the handoff, not the thinking. The next consumer (`bmad-create-epics-and-stories`) will hit three walls the PRD does not lower for it: a three-state tagging scheme (`[DELIVERED]` / `[NEW]` / untagged) that is only explained for one of the three states, no build sequencing anywhere in the PRD itself, and an Assumptions Index where five of seven entries have no inline tag to round-trip against. Separately, two substantive gaps deserve a paragraph each before build: FR-17 has no default resync interval and is therefore untestable as written, and the PRD defines "actual state" as what `ListRoutes` reads from Postgres — which quietly means the Operator can report `Converged` while the serving pod's routing table disagrees, a question the UJ-4 reviewer will ask in the first five minutes.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The decisions that got made are stated as decisions and they carry their costs. §7 rejects a PodDisruptionBudget with the actual mechanism (`minAvailable: 1` at one replica blocks drain forever, `maxUnavailable: 1` is no protection) rather than with a shrug. The Rust-operator rejection is routed to `addendum.md` A.1, which names the price out loud — "two toolchains, two test harnesses, two build paths in one Makefile" — instead of presenting the choice as free. FR-3's `[NOTE FOR PM]` sits at a genuine tension ("`GetGatewayStatus` cannot serve this purpose and must not be used for it"), not at a safe checkpoint. The five Open Questions are actually open: Q1 and Q4 are explicitly non-blocking, Q3 is routed to `bmad-architecture`, and none of them is answered in the following sentence. Nothing here "balances" anything.

Where it falls short of strong is the trade-off the PRD itself says is load-bearing and then never makes. §1.1 states plainly that "the artifact is only useful while the job search is live," and SM-C3 names *time before first push* as a counter-metric — but there is no time budget, no cut-line, and no statement of which of the nineteen unbuilt FRs goes first if the window compresses. §8.2 defers CI "if the timeline allows" against a timeline that does not exist in the document. For a project whose stated failure mode is a polished-but-unpushed repo, scope-versus-time is *the* decision, and it is the one decision the PRD leaves to be improvised at build time.

Relatedly, the PRD sets a counter-metric against its own surface area (SM-C1: "Adding surface area to the CRD or the Gateway API makes the artifact worse") and then never adjudicates any scope item against it. Is FR-27 (controller metrics) depth on the five ideas, or breadth? The counter-metric has no teeth while nothing in §8 is marked as the thing it would cut.

### Findings

- **high** No time budget on a PRD that says timing is load-bearing (§1.1, §8.2, SM-C3) — The document names the deadline risk three times and sizes it zero times. Nineteen unbuilt FRs with no ordering means the cut decision gets made under pressure, mid-build, with no prior agreement about what is expendable. *Fix:* add a short §8.3 "Cut order" naming the first three FRs to drop if the window compresses — FR-27, FR-12 and FR-23 are the natural candidates since none of them is one of the five ideas SM-3 tests — plus a target date for first push.
- **medium** SM-C1 has no enforcement surface (§9, §8.1) — A counter-metric that no scope item is measured against cannot fire. *Fix:* tag the two or three in-scope FRs that are breadth rather than depth, so SM-C1 has something concrete to counterbalance.
- **low** Q2 is a task, not an open question (§10.2) — "Pick during implementation and document the reasoning" is a decision assigned to the builder, not a question needing a decision-maker. Harmless, but it inflates the open-items count and dilutes the other four, which are real. *Fix:* move it into FR-17 as a build-time note.

## Substance over theater — strong

There is essentially no furniture in this document, and its absences are as telling as its contents. There is no personas section — correct for a solo builder, and the PRD reaches for "Jobs To Be Done" instead, which is the right instrument when the user count is one. There is no market sizing, no competitive landscape, and no differentiation section, none of which a template-filling PRD would have resisted adding.

The Vision passes the swap test decisively. §1's third paragraph — "an IaaS control plane **is** a reconciliation loop, and building one turns 'I wrote a Rust gRPC service' into 'I built a control plane with a reconciler, finalizers, and drift correction over a gRPC API'" — could not be lifted into another PRD without rewriting it, because it names the specific rhetorical move the artifact exists to make. §5's NFRs are likewise product-specific with real thresholds rather than the scalable/secure/reliable trio: a 4 GB RAM ceiling derived from a named host, "no external state cached across reconciles," "no sleeping inside Reconcile." FR-8's feature-specific NFR is an argument, not a boilerplate line — restarting a process because its dependency is down "converts an outage into a simultaneous restart storm across every replica."

The one place that reads thin is §4.8. FR-27's consequences ("Reconcile count, error count, and queue depth are scrapeable"; "the metrics port is declared on the pod spec") describe what `kubebuilder` scaffolds for free — this is scaffold output written up as a requirement. It is partially earned, because a reviewer reading the repo cold (UJ-4) does register a metrics endpoint as a signal, but the PRD should say that is why it is there.

### Findings

- **low** FR-27 is scaffold output presented as a requirement (§4.8) — Nothing in it requires a decision or an implementation choice; `controller-runtime` provides it by default. *Fix:* either state the UJ-4 legibility rationale explicitly, or fold FR-26 and FR-27 into one "Observability baseline" FR and reclaim the words.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD has a thesis and states it in §1 rather than leaving it to be inferred: the *shape* matters more than the feature list, because the target reader is an infrastructure engineer deciding in four minutes whether this person understands control planes. Everything downstream serves that. §4.5 Reconciliation is the centre of gravity and gets the most demanding requirements. §4.9 Repository Legibility exists explicitly because "an unreadable repo wastes the whole build" — a PRD that treats the README as load-bearing scope rather than documentation cleanup has understood its own thesis. UJ-2 is elevated to "the demo beat" and then traced through FR-17, FR-25, FR-30 and SM-1, which is a thesis reaching all the way into the acceptance criteria.

The Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity. SM-3 — "the five ideas are defensible unprompted... in conversation, from this implementation, without notes" — is precisely the thing the artifact exists to produce, and it is not a proxy for it. The counter-metrics are genuinely counter, not decorative: SM-C1 argues against the PRD's own surface area and SM-C2 rejects coverage percentage in favour of the named behaviours in FR-24/FR-25. A PRD willing to name test-coverage-chasing as a failure mode is not a backlog with section headings.

Scope kind is a coherent problem-solving/platform hybrid, and the scope logic matches: the delivered platform work is carried because the reconciler depends on its semantics, and the new work is almost entirely the loop plus the legibility that makes the loop visible.

### Findings

*(no findings — the thesis holds and the features follow from it; the one tension worth acting on, SM-C1 having no enforcement surface, is recorded under Decision-readiness)*

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This is the document's best dimension and the reason it will survive contact with story generation. Every one of the thirty FRs carries a **Consequences (testable)** block, and I went looking specifically for the weasel words this rubric says to flag — "gracefully," "reasonable performance," "user-friendly," "robust" — and found none. Bounds appear where adjectives normally live: "under 30 MB," "within ~15s," "**0 restarts**," "under ten seconds," "under 4 GB of usable RAM," "within one probe period."

Several consequences are sharper than typical acceptance criteria. FR-16's "A Tunnel already matching produces **no** gRPC write call — verified by call count, not by outcome" specifies the observation method, which is the difference between a test that passes for the right reason and one that passes by accident. FR-22's "removing any granted verb breaks a specific, identified operation" is a testable negative — a real check on RBAC over-grant rather than a claim of least privilege. FR-15's three-way converge case (absent, present-but-wrong, already correct) and FR-19's `observedGeneration`-lags-a-spec-edit case are both directly executable as `envtest` cases, and FR-24 already names them as required coverage.

One FR is genuinely untestable as written. FR-17's third consequence reads "The interval is configurable and defaults to a value documented in the README" — the default is deferred to Open Question 2, so there is no number to assert against. Its second consequence, "restored within one resync interval," is circular for the same reason: it is true for any interval whatsoever. Since FR-17 is the requirement that produces SM-1, the project's primary metric, this is the one hole worth closing before stories are cut. A secondary soft spot: FR-13 specifies the *mechanism* for `localIP` validation ("an API-server-enforced marker") but leaves `remoteEndpoint` as "validated as `host:port`" with no mechanism, so a story author will not know whether a CEL rule, a regex marker or Operator-side parsing is intended.

### Findings

- **high** FR-17 has no testable default and its restoration bound is circular (§4.5 FR-17, §10 Q2) — "defaults to a value documented in the README" asserts nothing, and "restored within one resync interval" is satisfied by any interval. FR-17 is the requirement SM-1 measures, so the project's primary metric currently rests on an unbounded FR. *Fix:* pick the default now (30s makes the demo beat land while staying gentle on the Gateway) and restate the consequence as a wall-clock bound: "restored within 30s of the out-of-band delete, with no cluster event."
- **low** FR-13 specifies a validation mechanism for one field and not the other (§4.4) — `localIP` names API-server enforcement; `remoteEndpoint` says only "validated as `host:port`". *Fix:* name the marker or CEL rule for `remoteEndpoint` too, and state whether rejection happens at `kubectl apply` as it does for `localIP`.
- **low** FR-28's "short, copyable command sequence" is the one unbounded adjective (§4.9) — Mitigated, because the binding criterion is "verified by actually running it on a clean environment," which is real. *Fix:* optional — a step count ("under eight commands") would make it checkable without the clean-machine run.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and reasoned, and in one case the PRD does something rare: §7 documents evidence *against* work it has already delivered. The NetworkPolicy non-goal states that the policies were written, are correct, and are not enforced — "verified directly: a pod reached Postgres despite an ingress policy denying it" — then explains why fixing it (Calico, no default CNI) does not fit the memory ceiling, and closes with the right principle: "An unenforced NetworkPolicy presented as protection is worse than none." I confirmed the manifest header in `k8s/40-networkpolicy.yaml` carries that disclaimer in the file itself, so the honesty is not confined to the PRD. §8.2's multi-replica entry is equally careful — "**This is deferred, not closed** — see §10 Q1" — which is the distinction most PRDs blur.

Open-items density is well proportioned to the stakes: five Open Questions, two inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, three `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts across thirty FRs, on a solo internal artifact. None blocks the start of build, and each is routed — Q3 to architecture, Q1 to a future replica change, the CI note to "if the timeline allows."

Two omissions, though, are reachable only by inference, and both are the kind an epic generator will silently assume away. The larger one is definitional. §3 Glossary defines **Actual state** as "the set of Tunnels the Gateway reports through **ListRoutes**," and FR-3 has `ListRoutes` read `vpn_routes` rather than the routing table. Combined with FR-16 ("already matching produces **no** gRPC write call"), this means the Operator's convergence check structurally cannot observe divergence between Postgres and the serving pod's in-memory table — so the Operator can report `Ready / Converged` while `RoutePacket` (FR-5) is serving a stale table. For MVP this is defensible, because the data plane is simulated and hydration runs at startup, but it is a *choice* and the PRD never presents it as one. Given that the thesis is drift correction and UJ-4's protagonist is an infrastructure engineer, this is the first question a sharp reviewer asks, and the PRD should own the answer rather than be caught by it.

The smaller one: §8.1 says the Helm chart replaces "the raw manifests," and Q5 asks which becomes the canonical entry point — but FR-12's consequences never require the `k8s/` manifests to be removed or re-pointed. As written, a story generator produces a Helm chart and leaves eight YAML files beside it, which is exactly the legibility cost Q5 is worried about.

### Findings

- **high** "Actual state" is defined as the durable record, so routing-table drift is invisible to the loop — and that choice is never stated (§3 Glossary, §4.1 FR-3, §4.5 FR-16) — The Operator can report `Ready / Converged` while the serving pod's in-memory table disagrees with `vpn_routes`, because nothing in the loop ever reads the routing table. Defensible for MVP given the simulated data plane, but currently a silent assumption at the exact point the PRD's thesis is strongest — and the obvious interview question against UJ-4. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or a §7 bullet: "The reconciler converges desired state against the durable record only. Divergence between `vpn_routes` and a pod's routing table is out of scope for MVP — hydration runs at startup and the data plane is simulated. Q1's periodic re-hydration is the fix if the Gateway ever becomes multi-replica."
- **medium** FR-12 never retires the raw manifests it replaces (§4.3 FR-12, §8.1, §10 Q5) — §8.1 says "replacing," the FR does not, and Q5 names the resulting two-entry-point problem without assigning an FR to resolve it. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-12 — "the raw `k8s/` manifests are removed and the README's quickstart points only at the chart" — and mark Q5 resolved by it.
- **low** No inline `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callouts anywhere — §7 and §8.2 carry the whole load. At this document length that works, but the two omissions above are precisely the ones that would have been caught by a callout at the point of inference (FR-3, FR-12) rather than a list at the end. *Fix:* place the two callouts above inline as well as in the lists.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The fundamentals are in place. The Glossary is present and — unusually — actually observed: Tunnel, Gateway, Operator, Drift, Resync and Reconcile are used as defined terms with consistent capitalisation across §4, §5 and §9, not drifting into lowercase synonyms. FR IDs run 1–30 contiguous with no gaps and no duplicates; UJ-1–4 and SM-1–5 plus SM-C1–C3 are likewise clean. I resolved every cross-reference in the document and all of them land on a defined target. Feature groups declare which UJs they realize, so the UJ→FR direction is traversable. The §4 grouping-with-global-numbering scheme described in §0 is the right call for exactly the reason given: regrouping features later will not renumber FRs.

What holds this at adequate rather than strong is that the specific consumer named in §0 — `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` — has to do three pieces of inference the PRD could have eliminated.

First, the tagging scheme has three states and explains one. §0 explains `[DELIVERED]`. FR-3 alone carries `[NEW]`. The other nineteen unbuilt FRs (FR-12 through FR-30) carry nothing at all. The natural misreading — that `[NEW]` marks the new work and therefore only FR-3 is outstanding — is the exact wrong answer, and it is the first question the epic generator must get right.

Second, there is no build order in the PRD. Real dependencies exist and are even stated inline in places ("relying on FR-2's idempotency," FR-23's "part of the same chart as the Gateway (FR-12)," FR-15's dependence on FR-3), but they are never collected. §8.1's in-scope list is in FR order, which is not build order — it puts Helm (FR-12) before the CRD exists (FR-13), and FR-23 before its own prerequisite. The dependency map does exist, in `addendum.md` §E, but it is framed there as a note-reconciliation aid and the PRD never points at it as the sequencing source.

Third, the Assumptions Index round-trips in one direction only. §11 lists seven entries; only two of them (§5's WSL2 host, §8.2's `replicas=1`) have a matching inline `[ASSUMPTION:…]` tag. The other five — `localIP` immutability, `ListRoutes` as a new RPC on the existing service, `kubebuilder` at `operator/`, Helm over Kustomize, and the load-bearing timing — are stated inline as settled fact. §0's claim that "Inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed in §11" is true but describes only half the contract. The practical cost: a story author reading FR-13 sees "`localIP` is immutable after creation" as a hard requirement to implement, with nothing signalling that it is an inference awaiting confirmation.

UJ protagonists are fine. UJ-1 through UJ-3 name Lilian and carry their context inline; UJ-4's "a reviewing engineer" is unnamed but role-specific and appropriate — no floating UJs.

### Findings

- **high** Three tagging states, one explained — the next consumer's first question is ambiguous (§0, §4 throughout) — `[DELIVERED]`, `[NEW]` (FR-3 only), and untagged (FR-12–FR-30) coexist with only the first defined. The plain reading of "`[NEW]` marks new work" gets nineteen FRs wrong. *Fix:* either tag all unbuilt FRs `[NEW]`, or drop the `[NEW]` tag from FR-3 and add one line to §0: "FRs without a `[DELIVERED]` tag are outstanding work."
- **medium** No build sequencing in the PRD (§8.1, addendum §E) — §8.1 lists scope in FR order, which inverts several real dependencies (FR-23→FR-12, FR-15/16→FR-3, FR-18→FR-2, FR-14→FR-13). The milestone map exists in `addendum.md` §E but is framed as a reconciliation aid, not as the sequencing source. Epic generation will re-derive this, probably differently. *Fix:* add a short §8.3 dependency list, or one line in §8.1 designating addendum §E as the authoritative build order.
- **medium** Assumptions Index round-trips only one way (§11 vs. §1.1, §4.1, §4.4, §4.3, §8.1) — Five of seven index entries have no inline tag and read as settled fact at their point of use. *Fix:* add inline `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags at FR-3, FR-12, FR-13 and §8.1, or mark those index entries as "stated inline without a tag" so the asymmetry is deliberate rather than accidental.
- **low** `[DELIVERED]` tags are internally inconsistent (§4.1 FR-4, FR-5; §4.1 FR-1) — §0 promises each `[DELIVERED]` FR carries "the milestone that shipped them," but FR-4 and FR-5 carry no milestone (reconciled only in addendum §E, which records them as pre-existing), and FR-1's tag is a date — "M0 session 2026-09-14" — where every other tag is a milestone id. *Fix:* use `[DELIVERED — pre-existing]` for FR-1, FR-4 and FR-5, and soften §0's promise accordingly.

## Shape fit — strong

The PRD has been fitted to what it actually is — an internal technical capability spec with a secondary reader — rather than forced into consumer-PRD clothing. The furniture that a template would have supplied is simply absent: no personas, no market sizing, no differentiation, no competitive analysis. §2 reaches for Jobs To Be Done instead of personas, which is the correct instrument when the user count is one, and §2.2 Non-Users does real work by ruling out the production/multi-tenant/team readings that would otherwise be assumed.

The interesting call is retaining UJs at all, since the rubric flags UJ density as overhead for a single-operator tool. Here they earn their place, and for a reason specific to this product: UJ-2 is not a usage narrative, it *is* the deliverable — the demo beat that SM-1 measures and FR-30 records. And UJ-4, the engineer reading the repo cold, is a genuine second audience that generates three FRs which would otherwise have no justification at all (FR-28 README, FR-29 ADRs, FR-30 recording). That is the inverse of UJ theater: the journeys are load-bearing enough that deleting UJ-4 would delete a whole feature group. Success Metrics are correspondingly operational and outcome-shaped rather than product-analytics — no DAU, no adoption curve — and SM-4 states the actual business outcome ("the CV line changes") plainly instead of dressing it up.

Brownfield handling is the strongest evidence of shape fit, and I verified it rather than taking it on trust. Every `[DELIVERED]` claim matches the repository: the `ON CONFLICT (local_ip)` upsert and the Postgres-delete-before-cache-removal ordering in `src/services/gateway.rs`; `DeleteTunnelResponse{success, existed}` in `proto/gateway.proto` with an idempotency comment in the proto itself; the `OVERALL = ""` / `LIVENESS = "liveness"` split in `src/services/health.rs`; both base images digest-pinned with `SQLX_OFFLINE=true` and `USER nonroot:nonroot` on distroless in the `Dockerfile`; `replicas: 1` plus all four hardening fields in `k8s/30-gateway.yaml`; `kindest/node:v1.33.1` with `hostPort: 50051` bound to `127.0.0.1` in `kind/cluster.yaml`; the exact action strings `FORWARDED`, `DROPPED (NO_ROUTE)` and `DROPPED (MALFORMED_HEADER)`; and every Makefile target FR-10 enumerates. `operator/` correctly does not exist. Delivered and new work are cleanly distinguished (modulo the tagging finding above).

On length: ~5,400 words against a 5–8 page target is an overshoot of roughly a third to a half. I judge it mostly earned. The bulk of the word count is the Consequences blocks, which are the document's entire downstream value — trimming them to hit a page count would gut the strongest dimension. The discipline is visible elsewhere, too: the technical-how, the rejected alternatives and the verification evidence were already moved to `addendum.md`, which is the split that keeps §4 readable. But the overshoot is not free, and it concentrates in three identifiable places. §0 Document Purpose spends ~220 words narrating structure to an audience of one plus a known skill. §1.1 and §2.1 substantially restate each other — both make the "unevidenced CV claim becomes a demonstrable artifact" point in different words. And three of the eleven `[DELIVERED]` FRs do not meet §0's own bar for inclusion: the justification is that "the operator's design depends on their exact semantics," which is plainly true for FR-1, FR-2, FR-7 and FR-8, and plainly not true for FR-5 (the reconciler never calls `RoutePacket`), FR-9 (image internals) or FR-11 (pod hardening). Those three could collapse into a single delivered-baseline bullet list at roughly 40% of their current length without costing the Operator's design anything.

### Findings

- **medium** Length overshoot is ~60% earned; the trimmable 40% sits in three known places (§0, §1.1/§2.1, §4.1 FR-5 / §4.3 FR-9, FR-11) — The Consequences blocks justify their words and should not be cut. §0's structural narration, the §1.1/§2.1 restatement, and three `[DELIVERED]` FRs that fail §0's own "the Operator depends on their semantics" test do not. The cost falls on UJ-4's four-minute reader and on the PM revisiting this in three weeks. *Fix:* collapse FR-5, FR-9 and FR-11 into one "Delivered platform baseline" list retaining only the consequences the Operator or the reviewer actually needs; cut §0 to three sentences; fold §1.1 into §2.1. Recovers ~900 words with no loss to story generation.
- **low** FR-5's consequence implies a request field that does not exist (§4.1 FR-5) — "A packet whose destination matches a Tunnel" reads as though destination is supplied, but `PacketRequest` carries only `payload` and the destination is parsed out of the IPv4 header. Imprecise rather than wrong. *Fix:* "A packet whose parsed IPv4 destination matches a Tunnel…" — or drop it with the consolidation above.
